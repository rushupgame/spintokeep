Guide #1

Local SEO for business owners: the 7 levers to rank first on Google Maps

When a customer types "bakery near me" or "hairdresser downtown", Google shows the local pack: three businesses featured on the map. Appearing there changes foot traffic radically. Ranking relies on three families of criteria: relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance; you can act on the other two.

Lever 1: a complete Google Business Profile

Exact primary category, secondary categories, up-to-date hours (including holidays), a rich description with natural keywords, services, products and recent photos. An incomplete listing is invisible.

Lever 2: review quantity

Reviews are the first prominence signal. Between two comparable businesses, the one with more recent, credible reviews is favored. The starting point of any strategy is a steady flow of reviews, not a one-off campaign.

Lever 3: freshness and regularity

Ten reviews a month for a year beat a hundred in one weekend followed by nothing. A QR code left permanently at the checkout guarantees that regularity without extra effort from your team.

Lever 4: rating and review content

A high average rating reassures, but the words used ("welcome", "speed", "haircut", "delivery") also tie your listing to specific searches. Encourage detailed reviews, never scripted ones.

Lever 5: replying to reviews

Replying to every review, positive or negative, shows you are serious, builds trust with future customers and adds relevant text to your listing.

Lever 6: photos and posts

Listings with authentic photos receive noticeably more direction requests and calls. Invite customers to share photos, and post your news every week.

Lever 7: consistent information (NAP)

Your name, address and phone number must be strictly identical across your website, social accounts, directories and Google.

QR codes in store: where to display, how to explain, how to measure

The QR code has become a reflex for most smartphone owners. But a badly placed QR code stays invisible: the success of a review campaign comes down to placement and wording details.

Measure to improve

Compare scans, participations and new reviews every week. If scans are low, move the poster; if participation is low, simplify the hook; if reviews are rare, check that the link goes straight to your listing.

Guide #3

Choosing prizes and calculating the ROI of an in-store contest

A well-chosen prize costs little, delights and brings the customer back. A badly chosen one costs a lot and produces nothing. Here is how to decide.

The 3 criteria of a good prize

Low real cost: favor high-margin products (coffee, pastry, syrup, samples). Immediate usefulness: the customer must understand what they win in a second. Deferred use: a prize to redeem on a next visit creates a reason to return.

Setting probabilities

The prize editor lets you set the probability of each prize. Reserve high-value prizes for very low probabilities and multiply small prizes with strong loyalty effects. You keep control of your game budget.

A simple ROI calculation

Track three indicators: the cost per participation (prize budget divided by players), the cost per review obtained and the value of a returning customer (average basket times annual frequency). With a €9.90 monthly subscription, a single extra returning customer is often enough to pay for the tool and the prize.

A compliant, lasting game

Display the rules, collect explicit consent for contacts and keep the review as an optional step. An honest game is a game that lasts and protects the credibility of your Google listing.

Guide #4

How to reply to Google reviews: templates for positive and negative reviews

Replying to reviews is not mere politeness; it is a local SEO and conversion lever. Each reply adds relevant text to your listing, proves you are active and reassures future customers, who often read replies before deciding. Aim for a reply within 48 hours, personalized and never copy-pasted.

Replying to a 5-star review

Thank the reviewer by first name, pick up a specific detail ("glad you loved the cut"), naturally mention your business and city, then invite them back. Example: "Thank you Camille! The whole team is delighted your facial treatment pleased you. See you soon at the spa."

Replying to a negative review

Never reply in the heat of the moment. Thank them for the feedback, apologize sincerely without over-justifying, offer to continue in private (phone or email) and say what you are improving. The public judges your professionalism more than the criticism itself: a well-handled negative review reassures.

Replying to a rating with no comment

A rating without text deserves a short, warm thank-you. It encourages the customer to return and to elaborate next time.

Mistakes to avoid

Identical automated replies, a defensive tone, disclosing personal information about the customer, unkept promises. Report clearly fake or off-topic reviews to Google rather than arguing.

Guide #5

Marketing gamification: why a prize wheel makes customers act

In-store gamification applies game mechanics (chance, reward, progression) to a commercial action. It works because it relies on simple, well-documented psychological drivers.

Reciprocity

When we receive something (a game, a gift, a pleasant moment), we naturally want to give back. Offering a playful experience creates a climate in which sharing a review or contact feels natural.

Uncertain reward

The anticipation of a possible win is more engaging than a guaranteed one. That is the principle of the spinning wheel, the slot machine or the scratch card: curiosity pushes people to the end of the journey.

Friction reduction

A 30-second game with no app and no heavy sign-up lowers the cost of action. The fewer the steps, the higher the share of customers who complete the journey.

Social proof

Reviews are themselves social proof: the more recent reviews your listing shows, the more visitors it converts into customers. Gamification feeds this virtuous circle.

Wheel, slot machine, scratch cards: which one?

The wheel is the most readable and visual in a shop window; the slot machine suits bars and festive venues; the scratch card fits high-frequency businesses. What matters is that the game stays simple, fast and true to your brand.

Guide #6

The 10 mistakes to avoid when collecting Google reviews in store

Many owners start enthusiastically, then give up for lack of results. Here are the most common mistakes and how to avoid them.

  1. Asking too late. After a few days the emotion has faded: ask on the spot.
  2. Hiding the QR code. A sign that is too small or too low goes unnoticed. Place it at eye level.
  3. Not telling the team. Without one invitation sentence at every checkout, participation drops.
  4. Pointing to the wrong page. Test the link: it must lead straight to your listing's review form.
  5. Making the prize conditional on a positive review. It goes against Google's rules and cheapens your rating.
  6. Not replying to reviews. An unanswered review is a missed chance to reassure.
  7. Offering a prize that is too expensive. A costly prize blows the budget without improving results.
  8. Forgetting GDPR consent. Without explicit opt-in, your contact base is unusable.
  9. Stopping after one month. Local SEO is built over time: regularity is key.
  10. Not measuring. Without tracking scans and participations, you cannot improve.

Guide #9

GDPR, rules and in-store contests: good practices

Collecting data and awarding prizes requires a simple but rigorous framework. This section gives general guidance and does not replace legal advice for your country and activity.

Explicit consent (opt-in)

The participant must clearly agree that their details may be used. An unticked checkbox with clear information is the reference practice.

Collect only what is necessary

Name, email and phone are enough for most businesses. The more data you ask for, the more you expose yourself and the higher the drop-off.

Retention period and rights

Set a reasonable period (for commercial prospecting, data protection authorities such as the CNIL in France generally refer to three years after last contact) and allow access, rectification and deletion on simple request.

Game rules

Write short rules (organizer, dates, prizes, procedure) and keep them available in store. Favor a game with no purchase necessary and check local regulations (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Madagascar).

Transparency about rewards

Never present the review as a condition for winning. This protects your Google listing and respects regulations on online reviews.

Glossary of local marketing and review collection

The key terms to master to manage your local visibility, online reputation and customer loyalty.

Local SEO

The techniques that improve your visibility in location-based searches ("bakery near me").

Local pack

The block of three businesses Google shows under the map, above regular results.

Google Business Profile

Formerly Google My Business: the free listing presenting your business on Google Search and Maps.

Google review

A rating and comment left by a customer on your listing, the first local prominence signal.

Social proof

The tendency to follow others' choices: recent reviews reassure and convert visitors.

Gamification

Using game mechanics (wheel, slot machine, scratch card) to encourage an action.

Loyalty QR code

A scannable code leading to a game or program and collecting qualified contacts.

A usable customer contact base for follow-ups, promotions and birthdays.

POS display

Point-of-sale advertising: the poster, stand or sticker carrying your QR code.

Conversion rate

The share of visitors or customers who take the expected action (scan, review, purchase).

Average basket

The average amount spent per visit, the basis for calculating a prize's ROI.

Online reputation

Your business image on the internet, built from reviews, photos and replies.

NAP

Name, address, phone: three details that must be identical everywhere on the web.

Customer loyalty

All actions that encourage a customer to return and spend more over time.

Frequently asked questions about collecting Google reviews with gamification

Everything you need to know to get more Google reviews, build customer loyalty and strengthen your local SEO.

How exactly does Spin To Keep work for my business?

It is very simple. Our platform turns the tedious task of collecting customer reviews into a fun interactive game. You design your digital game (wheel, slot machine) in a few clicks, set your prizes (discounts, goodies), and display your printed QR code in store. To try their luck, customers scan the code with their smartphone. After playing, they are automatically redirected to your review pages (Google Business Profile, Instagram, etc.).

Does this tool really help me get more positive Google reviews?

Yes, measurably. The main obstacle for a satisfied customer is forgetting: once outside the store, they no longer think about it. By offering a playful activity at the exact moment their satisfaction peaks, you remind them to leave a review without friction: one scan, one game, one direct link to your listing. Businesses moving from a simple flyer to gamification sharply increase their review flow (results vary with footfall and team involvement).

Can I customize the design of my promotional games?

Absolutely. To keep the digital experience consistent with your physical brand, our editor lets you easily import your logo and precisely change the colors of the games and buttons to match 100% of your brand guidelines.

How long does it take to set up the contest game?

Less than 5 minutes. The interface is designed for busy shop owners: you upload your logo, choose your colors, list your prizes, and your marketing campaign is instantly ready. The QR code is generated automatically.

Which platforms can I redirect winning customers to?

You have total freedom. You can of course configure the game to redirect to your Google Business Profile (for local SEO), but also to your Instagram or TikTok accounts, your shop's Facebook page, or even your TripAdvisor page if you are a restaurant.

What is the real price of the service? Are there hidden fees?

We offer full access to our SaaS platform for a single, transparent price of €9.90 incl. VAT per month. Unlike other solutions on the market, there is no hidden subscription and no setup fee. Once payment is validated, you immediately have access to all tools to launch your campaigns.

How do customers actually collect their prizes?

When a customer wins, the result is displayed on their phone. They simply show their screen at the checkout. You can validate the prize very quickly by checking the Dashboard, which prevents any fraud.

Do I need any particular technical or IT skills?

None at all. If you can use a basic smartphone to browse the internet, you can use Spin To Keep. We automatically generate the final QR code that you simply print or display on a tablet at the checkout.

How can I get more Google reviews in store?

You need three conditions: ask at the right time (right after the purchase or service), remove friction (a visible QR code at the checkout that opens your listing directly) and give a playful reason to act. A QR code game (prize wheel, slot machine) ticks all three boxes. Add a verbal reminder from your team and display the QR code at eye level: it is the simplest method to get more Google reviews at your point of sale.

How do I create a QR code to collect Google reviews?

With Spin To Keep, the QR code is generated automatically as soon as you have configured your game: you paste the link to your Google Business Profile as the destination, customize colors, logo and prizes, then download the print-ready poster. Allow about one minute. The customer scans, plays, then is invited to leave a review.

Can I offer a gift in exchange for a Google review?

Google's rules forbid buying or rewarding reviews, and regulations in many countries require disclosing any compensation. Best practice is to open the game to all customers, never make the prize conditional on a positive review and present the review as an optional step. The Spin To Keep journey lets customers continue without leaving a review. If in doubt about your situation, consult legal counsel.

What is the best contest idea to build customer loyalty in store?

Instant games via QR code work best because they need no equipment and play in 30 seconds. Pair them with a prize to redeem later (free coffee, 10% off the next visit, a free treatment): the win becomes a reason to return, and you collect name, email and phone with consent to follow up with your customers.

How many Google reviews do you need to rank well in local SEO?

There is no official threshold. Google combines the relevance, distance and prominence of your business, and prominence relies notably on the quantity, rating, freshness and regularity of reviews. A steady flow of new reviews each week beats a one-off spike: exactly what a QR code left permanently at the checkout provides.

Is a digital prize wheel GDPR-compliant?

Yes, provided data is collected with explicit consent (opt-in), collection is limited to what is necessary and the participant is informed of their rights of access, rectification and deletion. Spin To Keep includes this framework: notices and consent are built into the customer journey, and you export your base as CSV for your follow-ups.

How do I reply to a negative Google review?

Do not reply in the heat of the moment. Thank the reviewer for the feedback, apologize sincerely, offer to continue in private and say what you are improving. A calm, professional reply reassures future customers far more than an absence of criticism.

How long before I see the first results?

The first reviews arrive within days if the QR code is clearly visible and your team mentions it at the checkout. The effect on local ranking builds over several weeks, because the regularity of reviews matters as much as their number.

What is the difference between a prize wheel and a digital slot machine?

Both work through a QR code and are configured the same way: prizes, probabilities, colors. The wheel is very readable in a shop window and suits all businesses; the slot machine is more festive, appreciated by bars and entertainment venues.

How do I get more Google reviews for a restaurant?

Place a QR code in the bill holder, let the customer play while waiting to pay and invite them to share their experience. Then reply to every review. A weekly regularity in the review flow is the key.

Should I ask every customer for a review?

Yes, every customer, without filtering by satisfaction. Selecting only happy customers goes against Google's rules. Offering the game to everyone gives a faithful picture of your business and a credible rating.

What should I do about a fake review or one from a competitor?

Report it from your Google listing, explaining why it breaks the rules (non-existent customer, off-topic content, defamation). Reply factually and courteously while awaiting moderation.

How do I collect my customers' emails in store?

The simplest way is to offer a QR code game: the customer leaves their details with consent in exchange for a chance to win a prize. You then export the base as CSV for your newsletters and offers.

Can I create several games for several points of sale?

Yes, you can create several games and track them from your dashboard. Each point of sale can have its own prizes, design and review link.

How can I build customer loyalty without a loyalty card?

A game with a prize to redeem on the next visit effectively replaces the card: the customer has a reason to return, and you collect their details to follow up by email or SMS.

Why are Google reviews so important for a local business?

Because most customers read reviews before traveling. Reviews influence your position on Google Maps, your click-through rate and new customers' trust: they act on both visibility and conversion.
