How to get more Google reviews in your store: the 4-step method

Getting more Google reviews in your store is not a matter of luck, but of system. Businesses that receive a regular flow of reviews all apply the same logic: ask at the right moment, in the right place, with the right trigger.

2. Remove all friction

3. Give a fun reason to act

A marketing prize wheel turns a chore into a moment of fun. The game is open to everyone, the review remains an optional step, and the team no longer has to insist.

4. Measure and adjust

Scans, participations, prizes handed out: the dashboard shows you what works. Adjust your prizes and poster placement, then compare the number of new reviews each month.

Guide no. 1

Local SEO for shopkeepers: the 7 levers to show up first on Google Maps

When a customer types "bakery near me" or "hairdresser Lyon 3rd", Google shows the local pack: three businesses highlighted on the map. Being there radically changes foot traffic. Ranking rests on three families of criteria: relevance, distance and prominence. On distance, you can do nothing; on the other two, everything.

Lever 1: a complete Google Business Profile listing

Exact primary category, secondary categories, up-to-date opening hours (including holidays), a description rich in natural keywords, services, products and recent photos. An incomplete listing is invisible.

Lever 2: the number of reviews

Reviews are the first signal of prominence. Between two comparable businesses, the one with the most recent, credible reviews is favored. The starting point of any strategy is therefore a flow of reviews, not a one-off collection.

Lever 3: freshness and regularity

Ten reviews a month over a year are worth more than a hundred reviews in one weekend and then nothing. A QR code left permanently at the checkout ensures this regularity with no extra effort from the team.

Lever 4: the rating and the content of reviews

A high average rating reassures, but the words used ("welcome", "speed", "haircut", "delivery") also link your listing to specific searches. Encourage detailed reviews, never scripted ones.

Lever 5: replies to reviews

Replying to every review, positive or negative, shows you are serious, strengthens future customers' trust and adds relevant text to your listing.

Lever 6: photos and posts

Listings with authentic photos receive clearly more direction requests and calls. Invite your customers to share their photos and post your news every week.

Lever 7: consistency of your information (NAP)

Your name, address and phone number must be strictly identical on your website, social networks, directories and Google.

QR code at the point of sale: where to show it, how to explain it, how to measure it

Scanning a QR code has become a reflex for most customers with a smartphone. But a poorly placed QR code stays invisible: the success of a review campaign depends on details of placement and wording.

The 6 best places

  1. The checkout: a natural waiting moment, the customer has time to scan.
  2. The bill holder or receipt: perfect for restaurants.
  3. The table or counter: visible throughout the meal.
  4. The shop window: intrigues passers-by and creates a first contact.
  5. The bag or packaging: the game is played at home, with the same effect.
  6. The tablet at the checkout: ideal for the team to accompany the customer.

The sentence that gets people to scan

A short, concrete hook works better than an explanation: "Scan, play and try to win a gift!". Spin To Keep posters are print-ready, with your logo and colors, so the message reads at a glance.

Train the team in 5 minutes

Success depends on the team. A single sentence to remember, repeated at every checkout, is enough to multiply the number of participations. Avoid pushing the review: put the game first and let the review come naturally.

Measure to improve

Compare the number of scans, participations and new reviews every week. If scans are low, move the poster; if participation is low, simplify the hook; if reviews are scarce, check that the link goes straight to your listing.

Choosing prizes and calculating the ROI of an in-store prize game

A well-chosen prize costs little, delights a lot and brings the customer back. A poorly chosen one costs a lot and produces nothing. Here is how to decide.

The 3 criteria of a good prize

Low real cost: favor high-margin products (coffee, pastry, syrup, sample). Immediate usefulness: the customer must understand in a second what they win. Deferred use: a prize to use on a next visit creates a reason to return.

Balancing the odds

The prize editor lets you set the probability of each prize. Keep high-value prizes for very low odds and multiply small prizes with a big loyalty effect. This way you keep control of your promotion budget.

A simple ROI calculation

Keep three indicators in mind: the cost per participation (prize budget divided by number of players), the cost per review obtained and the value of a returning customer (average basket times annual frequency). With a €9.90 monthly subscription, a single extra customer who returns thanks to the game is often enough to pay for the tool and the prize.

A compliant and sustainable game

Display the rules, collect explicit consent for contacts and keep the review an optional step. An honest game is one that lasts and protects the credibility of your Google listing.

Guide no. 4

How to reply to Google reviews: reply templates for positive and negative reviews

Replying to reviews is not politeness, it is a local SEO and conversion lever. Each reply adds relevant text to your listing, proves you are active and reassures future customers, who often read the replies before deciding. Aim to reply within 48 hours, in a personalized way, without copy-pasting the same text.

Replying to a 5-star review

Thank them by first name, pick up a specific detail ("glad you liked the haircut"), naturally mention your business and your city, and invite them back. Example: "Thank you, Camille! The whole team is delighted you enjoyed your facial. See you soon at the salon."

Replying to a negative review

Never reply in the heat of the moment. Thank them for the feedback, apologize sincerely without over-justifying, offer to continue in private (phone or email) and say what you are improving. The public judges your professionalism far more than the criticism itself: a well-handled negative review reassures.

Replying to a review without a comment

A rating without text deserves a short, warm thank-you. It encourages the customer to come back and to detail their experience next time.

Mistakes to avoid

Identical automatic replies, a defensive tone, disclosing the customer's personal information, unkept promises. Report clearly fake or off-topic reviews to Google instead of arguing.

Guide no. 5

Marketing gamification: why a prize wheel gets your customers to act

In-store gamification means applying game mechanics (chance, reward, progression) to a commercial action. It works because it relies on simple, well-documented psychological drivers.

Reciprocity

When we receive something (a game, a gift, a pleasant moment), we spontaneously want to give something back. Offering a fun experience creates a favorable climate for sharing a review or a contact.

The uncertain reward

Waiting for a possible win is more attractive than a sure win. That is the principle of the spinning wheel, the slot machine or the scratch card: curiosity pushes people to the end of the journey.

Reducing friction

A 30-second game with no app and no heavy registration lowers the cost of action. The fewer the steps, the higher the share of customers who finish the journey.

Social proof

Reviews themselves are social proof: the more recent reviews your listing shows, the more visitors it converts into customers. Gamification feeds this virtuous circle.

Wheel, slot machine, scratch cards: which to choose?

The wheel is the most legible and visual in a shop window; the slot machine appeals to bars and festive venues; the scratch card suits high-footfall shops. What matters is that the game stays simple, quick and true to your image.

Guide no. 6

The 10 mistakes to avoid when collecting Google reviews at the point of sale

Many shopkeepers start with enthusiasm and then give up for lack of results. Here are the most frequent mistakes and how to avoid them.

  1. Asking too late. After a few days, the emotion has cooled: ask the customer on the spot.
  2. Hiding the QR code. A display that is too small or too low goes unnoticed. Place it at eye level.
  3. Telling the team nothing. Without an invitation sentence at each checkout, participation drops.
  4. Pointing to the wrong page. Test the link: it must lead straight to your listing's review form.
  5. Making the prize conditional on a positive review. It goes against Google's rules and devalues your rating.
  6. Not replying to reviews. An unanswered review is a missed chance to reassure.
  7. Offering a prize that is too expensive. A costly prize blows the budget without improving results.
  8. Forgetting GDPR consent. Without explicit opt-in, your contact database is unusable.
  9. Stopping after a month. Local SEO is built over the long term: regularity is the key.
  10. Not measuring. Without tracking scans and participations, improvement is impossible.

Guide no. 9

GDPR, rules and in-store prize games: best practices

Collecting data and handing out prizes requires a simple but rigorous framework. This section gives general guidance and does not replace legal advice tailored to your country and business.

Explicit consent (opt-in)

The participant must clearly agree to the use of their contact details. An unticked checkbox, with clear information, is the reference practice.

Collect only what is strictly necessary

Name, email and phone are enough for most shopkeepers. The more data you ask for, the more you expose yourself and the higher the drop-off rate.

Retention period and rights

Set a reasonable period (for commercial prospecting, CNIL recommendations usually mention three years from the last contact) and allow access, correction and deletion of data on simple request.

Game rules

Write short rules (organizer, dates, prizes, procedure) and keep them available in the store. Favor a game with no purchase required and check the applicable local rules (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Madagascar).

Transparency about consideration

Never present the review as a condition for winning. This protects your Google listing and complies with rules on online reviews.

Your business's image on the internet, built by reviews, photos and replies.

NAP

Name, address, phone: three details that must be identical across the web.

Loyalty

All the actions that encourage a customer to come back and spend more over time.

Frequently asked questions about collecting Google reviews through gamification

Everything you need to know to get more Google reviews, build customer loyalty and strengthen your local SEO.

How exactly does Spin To Keep work for my shop?

It's very simple. Our platform lets you turn the tedious collection of customer reviews into a very fun interactive game. You design your digital game (wheel, slot machine) in a few clicks, set the prizes to win (discounts, goodies) and display your printed QR code in the store. To try their luck, your customers scan the code with their smartphone. After playing, they are automatically redirected to your review pages (Google Business Profile, Instagram, etc.).

Does this tool really help me get more positive Google reviews?

Yes, and measurably. The main obstacle for a satisfied customer is forgetting: once outside the shop, they no longer think about it. By offering a fun activity at the exact moment their satisfaction peaks, you remind them to leave their review with no friction: one scan, one game, a direct link to your listing. Shopkeepers who move from a simple flyer to gamification strongly multiply their review flow (results vary with footfall and team involvement).

Can I customize the design of my promotional games?

Of course. So that the digital experience is perfectly consistent with your physical brand image, our editor lets you easily import your logo and precisely change the colors of the games and buttons to match your brand guidelines 100%.

How long does it take to set up the prize game?

Less than 5 minutes. The software interface is designed specifically for shopkeepers in a hurry: you upload your logo, choose your colors, list your gifts, and your marketing campaign is ready to use instantly. The QR code is generated automatically.

Which platforms can I redirect my winning customers to?

You have total freedom. You can of course set the game to redirect to your Google My Business listing (for local SEO), but also to your Instagram and TikTok accounts, your shop's Facebook page, or even your TripAdvisor page if you are a restaurant.

What is the actual price of the service? Are there hidden costs?

We offer full access to our SaaS platform at a single, transparent price of €9.90 incl. tax per month. Unlike other solutions on the market, there is no hidden subscription and no setup fee. Once payment is confirmed, you have immediate access to all the tools to launch your campaigns.

How do customers actually collect their prizes?

When a customer wins the game, their result is shown on their phone. They simply show the screen at the checkout. You can validate the prize very quickly by checking it in the Dashboard, which prevents any fraud.

Do I need any special technical or computer skills?

Absolutely none. If you can use a basic smartphone to browse the internet, you can use Spin To Keep. We automatically generate the final QR code, which you just need to print or display on a tablet at the checkout.

How do you get more Google reviews in store?

Three conditions are needed: ask at the right moment (right after the purchase or service), remove friction (a visible QR code at the checkout that opens your listing directly) and give a fun reason to do it. A QR code activity (prize wheel, slot machine) meets all three. Add a verbal reminder from the team and place the QR code at eye level: it is the simplest method to get more Google reviews at the point of sale.

How do you create a QR code to collect Google reviews?

With Spin To Keep, the QR code is generated automatically as soon as you have configured your game: you paste your Google Business Profile listing link as the destination, customize colors, logo and prizes, then download the print-ready poster. Allow about a minute. The customer scans, plays and is then invited to leave their review.

Can you offer a gift in exchange for a Google review?

Google's rules forbid buying or rewarding reviews, and French regulations require disclosing any consideration. Good practice is to open the game to all your customers, never make the prize conditional on a positive review and present the review as an optional step. The Spin To Keep journey lets people continue without leaving a review. If in doubt about your situation, consult a legal advisor.

What is the best prize game idea to build customer loyalty in store?

Instant QR code games work best because they require no equipment and are played in 30 seconds. Pair them with a prize to redeem later (free coffee, -10% on the next visit, free treatment): the win becomes a reason to come back, and you collect name, email and phone with consent to re-contact your customers.

How many Google reviews do you need to rank well in local SEO?

There is no official threshold. Google combines the relevance, distance and prominence of your business, and prominence rests, among other things, on the number, rating, freshness and regularity of reviews. A continuous flow of new reviews each week is better than a one-off spike: this is exactly what a QR code left permanently at the checkout provides.

Is a digital prize wheel GDPR-compliant?

Yes, provided data is collected with explicit consent (opt-in), collection is limited to what is necessary and the participant is informed of their rights of access, correction and deletion. Spin To Keep includes this framework: notices and consent are built into the customer journey, and you export your database in CSV format for your follow-ups.

How do you reply to a negative Google review?

Don't reply in the heat of the moment. Thank them for the feedback, apologize sincerely, offer to continue in private and say what you are improving. A calm, professional reply reassures future customers far more than the absence of criticism.

How long does it take to see the first results?

The first reviews arrive within the first few days if the QR code is clearly visible and the team mentions it at the checkout. The effect on local ranking builds over several weeks, because the regularity of reviews counts as much as their number.

What is the difference between a prize wheel and a digital slot machine?

Both work through a QR code and are set up the same way: prizes, odds, colors. The wheel is very legible in a shop window and suits every business; the slot machine is more festive and appeals to bars and entertainment venues.

How do you get more Google reviews for a restaurant?

Place a QR code in the bill holder, have the customer play while waiting to pay and invite them to share their experience. Then reply to every review. The weekly regularity of the review flow is the key.

Do you have to ask every customer for a review?

Yes, every one, without filtering by satisfaction. Selecting only happy customers goes against Google's rules. Offering the activity to everyone gives a true picture of your business and a credible rating.

What should you do about a fake review or a competitor's review?

Report it from your Google listing, explaining why it breaks the rules (non-existent customer, off-topic content, defamation). Reply factually and politely while waiting for moderation.

How do I collect my customers' emails in store?

The simplest is to offer a QR code activity: the customer leaves their details with consent in exchange for a chance to win a prize. You then export the database as CSV for your newsletters and offers.

Yes. The game opens in the browser, on both smartphone and tablet, with no app to install. You can also display the QR code on a tablet at the checkout.

Can I create several games for several points of sale?

Yes, you can create several games and track them from your dashboard. Each point of sale can have its own prizes, design and review link.

How do you build customer loyalty without a loyalty card?

A game with a prize to redeem on the next visit effectively replaces the card: the customer has a reason to come back, and you collect their details to re-contact them by email or SMS.

Why are Google reviews so important for a local business?

Because most customers check reviews before traveling. Reviews influence your position on Google Maps, your click-through rate and new customers' trust: they act on both visibility and conversion.
